Real-World Problem & Practical Scenarios

Why traditional expense trackers fail human relationships and how FairShare solves them

⚠️ Flaws of Centralized Splitwise & Excel

  • Unilateral Entry: One roommate enters an arbitrary ₹4,000 grocery bill; everyone is charged before anyone can review the receipt.
  • Silent History Deletion: In spreadsheets or apps, transactions can be altered or erased months later without proof.
  • Doctored Invoices: Receipts uploaded off-chain can be photoshopped with zero cryptographic proof of tampering.
  • Circular Debt Churn: Round-trip payments (A owes B, B owes C, C owes A) cause unnecessary friction and banking fees.
  • Shared Device Impersonation: On a shared laptop, anyone can click "Approve" pretending to be a flatmate.

🌍 5 High-Impact Real-World Scenarios

  • 1. Apartment Roommates: Rent, Wi-Fi, electricity, groceries. Debts only take effect after the group consensus agrees.
  • 2. Friends Group Vacations: Flights, Airbnb, rental cars, dining. Min-Cash-Flow reduces 25 circular IOUs into 2 final payments.
  • 3. Hackathon & College Teams: Shared server hosting (AWS/OpenAI APIs) & hardware with transparent admin approvals.
  • 4. Freelance Collectives & DAOs: Instant atomic mock stablecoin (FSUSD) payouts on Base Sepolia blockchain.
  • 5. Corporate Petty Cash & Clubs: Non-deletion audit trails satisfy club advisers and university tax auditors.

⚙️ How It Works & Real-World Use Case

  • Frozen Balances: Unlike traditional apps that immediately debit everyone's account, FairShare holds the transaction in an escrowed block state.
  • Quorum Thresholds: Under Majority Vote, as soon as Charlie votes "Agree", the contract executes approveExpense() and permanently updates ledger balances.
  • Objection Protection: If Dana clicks "Reject & Object", she logs a formal reason ("I was traveling all March"). The block enters DISPUTED state and halts execution.
  • Real-World Use Case: Prevents a roommate from logging an inflated utility bill without roommates vetting and approving their exact portion.

⚙️ How It Works & Real-World Use Case

  • Web Crypto API SHA-256: The file is read into memory as an ArrayBuffer. crypto.subtle.digest computes a tamper-proof 256-bit cryptographic digest before any network call.
  • Storage Cost Optimization: Only the 32-byte hash and simulated IPFS CID are committed to the blockchain, minimizing gas costs while securing mathematical proof of invoice integrity.
  • Flexible Split Calculations: Automatically calculates exact decimal splits or percentage breakdowns with instant balance validation.
  • Real-World Use Case: If a roommate later edits the PDF invoice in Photoshop, the SHA-256 hash immediately changes, catching doctored bills instantly.

Min-Cash-Flow Settlement Hub

Graph debt simplification algorithm + atomic crypto & off-chain UPI settlements

🖥️ Current Frontend Appearance

MIN-CASH-FLOW OPTIMIZED DIRECT SETTLEMENT PLAN 8 DEBTS → 2 PAYMENTS
Bob owes Alice: ₹1,200.00
⚡ Pay with FSUSD (Crypto) 📱 Pay UPI / Cash
Charlie owes Dana: ₹650.00
⚡ Pay with FSUSD (Crypto) 📱 Pay UPI / Cash
🎉 Canvas Confetti celebration animation triggers upon successful settlement!

⚙️ How It Works & Real-World Use Case

  • Graph Reduction Engine: Solves the $N$-party circular debt problem. Computes net balances for each person, then iteratively pairs the maximum debtor with the maximum creditor.
  • Dual-Mode Settlement:
    • Atomic On-Chain Crypto: Transfers mock stablecoin (FSUSD) directly on Base Sepolia. Balances auto-settle upon transaction receipt.
    • Off-Chain UPI / Cash: Payer enters their UPI Tx ID; payee clicks "Confirm Received" to settle the ledger.
  • Real-World Use Case: After a 5-person week-long trip with 30 separate expenses, members settle the entire vacation with just 2 or 3 direct payments.

⚙️ How It Works & Real-World Use Case

  • Why No Delete Button? Deleting financial transactions destroys accountability and invites disputes. In FairShare, records can only be corrected, never deleted.
  • Parent-Child Delta Chains: correctExpense() flags the original record as CORRECTED and creates a child block with the updated shares and an ancestor pointer.
  • Mandatory Re-Voting: The newly corrected block must be re-approved by the group's quorum policy before ledger balances are adjusted.
  • Real-World Use Case: At the end of a lease, roommates have an undeniable audit trail of every adjustment made throughout the year.
